Best Audio Tag Editor Apps for Android (2026): Fix Album Art & ID3 Metadata

Nothing ruins a pristine local music collection faster than missing album covers, "Unknown Artist" tags, and garbled track numbers. Discover the best Android tools to edit audio metadata completely offline.

BG
ByteGif Editorial
Mobile & Audio Team
Verified 2026 Guide

When you download music from independent band websites, rip personal CDs, or transfer audio files from your computer to your Android phone, media players rely entirely on embedded metadata tags (such as ID3v2 for MP3s, Vorbis comments for FLAC/OGG, and MP4 atoms for M4A/AAC).

If these tags are corrupted or missing, your music player turns into chaos: songs from the same album split into five separate albums, track titles read like track_01_final_v2.mp3, and album artwork defaults to an ugly grey placeholder icon.

A dedicated audio tag editor lets you clean up your entire music library on your phone without needing a desktop computer. We tested the top Android tag editors for codec support, batch editing, privacy, and album art embedding. Here are the best audio tag editors in 2026.

2. Star Music Tag Editor — Fast Online Metadata Lookup

Star Music Tag Editor is one of the most widely used taggers on Android. Its standout feature is its quick internet search integration: if you have an unlabelled track, you can tap the search icon to fetch song titles, artist names, and album artwork from web databases.

It allows you to edit lyrics, customize album art by cropping images on the fly, and modify files stored on external SD cards.

3. AutomaTag — Automated Acoustic Fingerprinting

If your collection has hundreds of completely unnamed audio files (e.g. audio_0491.mp3), manually typing titles is impractical. AutomaTag incorporates acoustic fingerprinting technology similar to Shazam.

It analyzes a short sample of the actual audio waveform, identifies the song in an online database, and automatically updates the title, artist, year, and high-definition cover art with minimal manual input.

4. Kid3 Audio Tagger — Open-Source Technical Powerhouse

For audiophiles who demand precise control over tag standards (converting between ID3v1.1, ID3v2.3, and ID3v2.4), Kid3 is a legendary open-source tool.

It features powerful batch file renaming based on tags (e.g. automatically renaming files to %{track} - %{artist} - %{title}.flac) and extracting tags directly from directory structures.

Why ID3v2.3 vs. ID3v2.4 Matters for Music Compatibility

Pro Tip: Avoiding Split Albums in Car Stereos

If your car infotainment system or older portable music player fails to show album art, it is usually because the file was tagged using ID3v2.4 with UTF-8 encoding, which many hardware stereos do not support.

Using Audio Tag Editor to write standardized ID3v2.3 tags with embedded JPEG album art guarantees 100% compatibility across Android Auto, Apple CarPlay, smart TVs, and vintage MP3 players.
